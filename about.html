Our origin

A foundation is just a promise someone has to keep.

In 2009 our founders — a hydrologist, a teacher and a logistics manager — watched a $40,000 hand-pump fail in Mchinji because nobody local had the spare parts budget to fix it. The pump lasted nine weeks. The organisation they built has lasted sixteen years.

  • Maintenance is not overhead

    We budget 18% of every water project for the first five years of repairs. It is the line item we are proudest of.

  • We publish our failures too

    34 projects have been paused or closed since 2010. All 34 are documented in our annual review, with the reasons.

  • No celebrity ambassadors

    Appearances cost money that could drill a borehole. We spend that money on boreholes instead.

Milestones

Sixteen years, told straight.

2010

Eleven families, one pump

Hearth is incorporated as a 501(c)(3) in Oregon with $6,400 in savings and a donated set of drill bits. The first borehole serves eleven households in Malawi.

2013

The maintenance principle

After a $40,000 pump dies in nine weeks, we restructure every project around a five-year maintenance fund and a trained local water committee. The model has not changed since.

2016

Rapid Response is born

A cyclone response arrives late because supplies were bought after the storm. We build pre-positioned warehouses and hit our first 72-hour target in Odisha.

2019

Quarterly open books

We become one of the first mid-size US nonprofits to publish bank statements, executive compensation and every sub-grant above $1,000 — unedited, every ninety days.

2022

Solar Health Hubs

Cold-chain failures were costing clinics more vaccines than theft ever did. Ninety-two off-grid solar installations later, spoiled stock is near zero.

2026

2.4 million reached

212 community boards across 18 countries. Programmatic spend reaches 84 cents on the dollar, and 73% of the programme budget now sits outside the United States.

What guides us

Three commitments, written into the bylaws.

These are not values on a wall. Each one is enforceable — two of the three can be used by a donor to remove the board.

Publish the uncomfortable

Missed targets, failed audits, projects we abandoned. If it would embarrass us in a headline, it goes in the annual review anyway.

Stay after the ribbon

Every programme carries a ten-year maintenance commitment. We do not celebrate a handover as if the work were finished.

Move money fast

Donor funds reach field partners within 30 days of receipt. Our own payroll is the slowest thing we pay.

Governance

Who is actually in charge.

An eleven-person board with a rotating majority drawn from the communities we serve. Four independent committees — finance, safeguarding, audit and programme quality — publish their own minutes.
